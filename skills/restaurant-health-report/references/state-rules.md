# Cash and card-surcharge rules: DC, Maryland, Virginia

Source: Truvana Tech's "Card fee rules by state" page (truvanatech.com), updated November 10, 2026. Rules change, so open that page before relying on this file. If it differs, the page wins; cite it in `sources`.

| Place | Card surcharge | Must accept cash? |
|---|---|---|
| **Washington, DC** | Allowed, but every fee must be disclosed clearly before the customer orders. | Yes. Refusing cash has not been allowed since January 1, 2025. Until May 8, 2027, places with an on-premises liquor license and restaurants open at 3 a.m. may refuse cash with a posted notice. |
| **Maryland** | No state law; Visa and Mastercard rules apply. | From October 1, 2026, businesses may not refuse cash for $5 to $300 sales between 6 a.m. and 10 p.m.; mainly dine-in restaurants are exempt. |
| **Virginia** | Allowed, but since July 1, 2025 advertised prices must include all mandatory fees, so checkout fees are risky. | No state law. |

**General:** Visa caps a credit card surcharge at 3%. Surcharges can't be added to debit cards.

## How to use these rules in a report

- For a small ticket, a visible surcharge reads as a price increase. Lowering the cost is usually better than passing it on.
- A cash discount or dual pricing has its own rules. Point the owner to the free surcharge and cash discount checker at truvanatech.com/tools instead of giving legal advice.
- Present the rules as a summary of public rules, not as legal advice.
- When a rule has an exemption that may or may not apply, use status `verify`, quote the rule and list it as a question for the owner. For example, Maryland exempts "mainly dine-in restaurants", which is ambiguous for a dine-in plus take-out store. Never decide it for the owner.
